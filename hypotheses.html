01
RANK 1 · FIRST TO SCREENREGIONAL COVERAGE NOT CHECKED

3D hypocenter planes → independent surface-fault candidates

Layers / source
Relocated Nevada/nearby earthquake catalogue from Trugman (2024) and the open Zenodo release. Audit the actual field schema and quality metadata first; use only confirmed coordinates/depth/time fields, and compare event-time subsets only if those fields are present and trustworthy.
Physical signature
Robust, spatially coherent planar event families with consistent strike/dip and a plausible depth extent; project the fitted planes toward the near-surface trace with uncertainty propagated, rather than merely rasterizing epicenter density.
Why it could be new
Subsurface fault-plane geometry can reveal structural continuation or a fault not represented by a surface catalogue. This is a different proposed transform from 2-D earthquake-count density, though event catalogues may already be used elsewhere in the shared pipeline.
Non-fault mimic
Aftershock/induced seismicity clusters, volcanic or dike-related events, mining blasts, and poorly located events can form apparent planes. Depth and time coherence alone do not prove a surface fault trace.
Novelty in this repo
No feature code or registry raster exists here; distinctness versus the active template is unverified. Must pass every-raster correlation and 3-pixel overlap gates before and after placement.
Source/access
Zenodo record DOI 10.5281/zenodo.11167510 is listed open under CC-BY-4.0; it names `nvreloc_catalog_newmag.txt` and `station_terms.txt` (about 13.8 MB combined). The associated 2024 paper describes a Nevada/nearby catalogue spanning 2008–2023. Files/schema, exact AOI event coverage, and quality fields have not been audited locally.
Cost / DTI outlook
Medium to high. Relocation QC, robust 3-D clustering/plane fits, uncertainty propagation and surface projection are required. Highest qualitative priority of this shortlist, but expected DTI is unknown; no numeric forecast.
First falsification
Feature-alone blocked-fold test; exclude synthetic/induced and poorly constrained clusters; compare geometric plane feature against event-density-only and incumbent using the exact same holdout and plausible truth surfaces.
02
RANK 2 · SCREEN SOURCE METADATAFILE, TERMS & AOI OPEN

Airborne magnetics and radiometrics → concealed structural breaks

Layers / source
USGS/DOE GeoDAWN airborne magnetic and radiometric grid GeoTIFFs (Area 1/Area 2), with flight-line and survey-outline metadata. Candidate transforms include scale-aware gradients, edge continuity and cross-band lineament agreement; inspect actual raster/field metadata before use.
Physical signature
Repeated, spatially coherent linear gradient breaks or offsets across magnetic anomalies and/or radiometric boundaries, supported across bands and separated from flight lines and seams. Such structures can mark faults that juxtapose or offset lithologies where surface catalogues lack a trace; geophysics is not fault-specific.
Why it could be new
Subsurface magnetic/radiometric contrasts may reveal structural discontinuities beneath cover that are not represented by a surface-fault catalogue. This differs from fault-distance and event-density transforms, but may overlap other geophysical features already in the shared stack.
Non-fault mimic
Lithologic contacts, intrusions or dikes, hydrothermal alteration, soil/moisture/vegetation effects on radiometry, flight-line striping or leveling errors, interpolation edges and acquisition seams can mimic a fault-aligned signal.
Novelty in this repo
No geophysical feature code, feature inventory or raster registry is present. Conceptual novelty is plausible but distinctness versus the active shared stack and every registry raster is unverified.
Source/access
Official USGS GeoDAWN record (DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ) describes two survey areas and lists Area 1/Area 2 GeoTIFF grid ZIPs plus extent outlines. No bytes were downloaded or checksummed locally; usage terms, grid metadata and exact overlap with the competition polygon remain unchecked. Source listing alone does not make this test-ready.
Cost / expected benefit
Medium to high. Verify terms and footprint, inspect/resample grids, remove survey artifacts, derive multiscale features and run spatial folds. Qualitative potential is moderate if coverage and independent signal are confirmed; expected DTI is unknown, not projected.
First falsification
Confirm terms, inspect official outlines against the exact AOI, and audit units/CRS/nodata before processing. Test each source band alone, use non-fault negative controls, then compare on the buffered holdout and at least 32 plausible truth rasters with the exact mean marginal-DTI rule.
03
RANK 3 · COARSE INDEX HITSEXACT AOI OVERLAY OPEN

High-resolution terrain → drainage deflection and channel offsets

Layers / source
USGS 3DEP lidar-derived DEMs and coverage/elevation indexes. Derive drainage networks, channel direction/curvature, local relief and lineament-aligned offsets automatically at suitable scales.
Physical signature
Repeated stream deflection, aligned channel elbows, offset or beheaded channels, and systematic slope breaks crossing a plausible fault corridor. Multi-channel coherence is more persuasive than one sharp topographic edge.
Why it could be new
Fault slip and differential uplift can disrupt drainage and create geomorphic offsets not encoded in a fault catalogue. This targets surface geomorphic response, not simple elevation/slope or a fault-distance buffer.
Non-fault mimic
Lithologic contacts, resistant beds, landslides, alluvial-fan boundaries, roads/ditches and natural channel meanders can mimic aligned breaks or deflections.
Novelty in this repo
Terrain derivatives and prior rasters are absent. The repository cannot establish whether an equivalent geomorphometric feature is already in the shared stack; registry comparison is mandatory.
Source/access
USGS 3DEP 1-meter DEM collection and National Map elevation index. A 2026-10-11 query of the index's 1-meter project layer against the GeoDAWN record's coarse bounding rectangle returned 22 project polygons—not 22 tiles and not an exact GEMS-polygon overlay. Target coverage, resolution, quality and download size remain open. The published Tahoe/Sierra USGS lidar study is only a regional analogue.
Cost / DTI outlook
High. Tile coverage screening, mosaicking, hydrologic conditioning and scale-aware channel extraction. Qualitative potential: moderate if adequate lidar exists; otherwise low. DTI is unknown, not projected.
First falsification
Check coverage before processing. Build only deterministic terrain derivatives; mask water/roads where data permit; test each derivative alone and against the incumbent on buffered whole-segment folds.

RANK 4 · SCENE/ACCESS GATE FIRSTAOI SCENE STACK UNKNOWN

Sentinel-1 InSAR → persistent deformation-gradient structures

Layers / source
Copernicus Sentinel-1 SAR acquisitions and scripted interferometric time-series or coherence products, accessed through the official Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem. Use only a consistently processed, quality-screened time interval.
Physical signature
Persistent, spatially coherent lineaments in displacement gradients or decorrelation that cross a plausible fault zone and repeat across independent acquisitions; distinguish a gradient from broad atmospheric ramps.
Why it could be new
Active creep, subsidence across a structure, or deformation linked to geothermal fluid movement may reveal a currently active fault signal independent of static surface mapping.
Non-fault mimic
Seasonal soil moisture, groundwater withdrawal, geothermal production/injection, volcanic deformation, atmospheric delay, decorrelation, orbit artefacts and processing seams can all produce apparent gradients.
Novelty in this repo
No SAR stack or feature inventory is present. It differs conceptually from a static lineament map but may duplicate deformation layers already available in a shared stack; this is untested.
Source/access
The official Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem describes free access to Copernicus data; an AOI acquisition query and account test were not performed. ESA's land-deformation overview is background only. Do not treat this as test-ready until repeatable AOI/time coverage is confirmed.
Cost / DTI outlook
Very high. Scene screening, phase unwrapping/time series, atmospheric correction and quality control. Qualitative near-term potential is low until a stable AOI stack is confirmed; DTI is unknown.
First falsification
Automate an AOI/date coverage inventory first. Require repeatability across acquisitions and negative-control non-fault areas, then run a feature-alone spatial holdout test. Stop if the signal follows known pumping or atmospheric artefacts.
05
RANK 5 · DATA RETRIEVAL BLOCKEDNO GDR SUBMISSION FOUND

INGENIOUS geochemistry and paleogeothermal deposits → concealed fluid pathways

Layers / source
Where reliably available: georeferenced hot-spring/well chemistry, temperature, silica/carbonate sinter or tufa indicators, alteration observations and related INGENIOUS/DOE geothermal data. Keep each source/layer distinct before any combination.
Physical signature
Aligned thermal/geochemical anomalies or paleogeothermal deposits that imply focused fluid discharge along a persistent structural corridor; use spatial continuity and independent chemistry rather than proximity to any single spring.
Why it could be new
Mineral deposits and geochemical discharge can preserve evidence of older fluid flow and structures where modern topographic expression is weak, supplementing rather than simply copying a mapped-fault layer.
Non-fault mimic
Stratigraphic permeability, lithologic contacts, volcanic heat, isolated springs or wells, alteration unrelated to a mapped surface trace, and uneven sampling can create aligned-looking anomalies.
Novelty in this repo
No geochemistry or prior registry is present. Conceptual novelty is plausible relative to a fault-only catalogue but not established relative to the shared stack or sibling-project outputs.
Source/access
The OSTI INGENIOUS record (DOI 10.15121/1881483) describes relevant data themes and indicates attribution is required for redistribution subject to posted terms. The linked GDR 1391 record returned “No submission found” on 2026-10-11. Actual usable files, file-level terms and spatial completeness are unverified; do not build on this record until retrieval succeeds.
Cost / DTI outlook
High / blocked. Data harmonization and sampling-bias controls are substantial, and source retrieval is unconfirmed. Do not spend an experiment until the dataset is accessible, licensed and spatially validated. DTI is unknown.
First falsification
Verify source files and license, audit sampling footprint, then test each observation type independently. Use spatial block splits that separate whole source clusters; do not infer unsampled areas as absence.
