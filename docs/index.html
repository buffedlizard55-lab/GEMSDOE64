Why this lane, and why it is different

Read the full research →
01 / THE GAP

The 100 m stack throws the scarps away.

A 100 m cell is 10 000 m². A fresh fault scarp is often 1–3 m of relief over 10–30 m of ground: real, mappable, and invisible once averaged. Bare-earth lidar still carries it, and this project's lane already holds the 100 m aggregate of those channels.

02 / THE ALGEBRA

Mass is not the game.

With DTI = T / (0.2·T + 0.8·Ng + 0.2·FP) every term is linear in the prediction, so a dot pays only if its marginal kernel credit beats 0.2 × DTI. The sibling project's five-rung ladder (121 131 → 60 069 → 44 090 → 40 199 → 37 654 dots, 0.1922 → 0.2778) is a pruning ladder: its field had saturated, so each later rung paid by removing mass.

03 / THE MOVE

Re-rank with the lane, then cover.

A blocked HistGradientBoosting field over 122 lidar-scarp, topographic, magnetic, radiometric, strain and basin-depth channels — held-out super-block AUC 0.792 — followed by a lazy-greedy selector that maximises expected kernel coverage instead of spending two dots on the same ground.
