Why h33-h33-2-b2…-e5eb6e7e-zeros tops the family — and what beating it requires

The one-line metric

DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w) with a triangular kernel of radius 3 px (300 m) and FN_w = |G| − TP_w. False positives are charged 0.2 per unit of emitted mass; missed truth is charged 0.8. Written with a = A/|G| (the credited fraction of hidden truth) and N emitted dots, the corrected closure used by the sibling analysis is DTI = a / (0.8 + 0.2·N/|G|).

Three mechanisms, in the order they were measured

  1. Dotting (emission geometry). A dot on a fault line credits up to 3 px of truth (the kernel area) while costing 0.2 when it misses. Measured live by the owner group: solid ridge 0.1922 (121,131 px) → thinned at 1.5 px 0.2477 (60,069 px) → thinned at 2.8 px 0.2600 (44,090 px). On a 1-D fault crest, re-spacing at 2.8 px retained 0.9063 of the credited mass while removing 26.6 % of the dots.
  2. Catalogue-flank pruning. The h33 file is the group's 40,199-dot base (GEMS28-H27-4-R1-SOLO-D2.8, owner-reported live 0.2708) minus every dot within 2 px (200 m) of the published catalogue → 37,654 dots. The group's instruments (four spatial quadrants, mirrored live score, safety factor 2.08) attributed +0.00487 to it and projected 0.2747; the owner-reported live value quoted for it is 0.2778. Both numbers are owner-reported; no organizer receipt was seen in any repository checked here.
  3. Surface quality. The same pruning applied to a weaker surface does not reproduce the gain, so the detector behind the dots carries the rest of the signal.

What 0.32+ requires (sibling closure, recomputed here)

At the sibling's calibrated hidden-truth size (|G| ≈ 12.2k px) and credited fraction a ≈ 0.386, reaching 0.3195 needs N ≈ 25k dots instead of 44k — the same credited mass concentrated in half the dots — or, at the current dot count, a +24 % increase in credited mass. Both are ranking problems (which pixels to keep), not packing problems: re-spacing alone cannot supply them. That is exactly the hypothesis this run tests, and the reason the emission here is a binary dot set with an explicit 2.8 px caliper and a catalogue-flank rule.

Can this beat 0.2778?

Two honest answers. On the surrogate instrument, yes with a large margin against the group's best live-scored surface — but that instrument is not the hidden test set. Against the real leaderboard, unproven: the current top of the public leaderboard is 0.4133 (leaderboard, snapshot verified 2026-10-11), and the gap between 0.28 and 0.41 is a detector problem, not an emission problem.
