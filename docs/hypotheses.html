Five hypotheses, ranked; three were runnable here

H1 — Strike-coherence veto on lidar scarp channels implemented & measured

Layers: the 1 m 3DEP lidar-derived scarp layer (or the 1 m DEM itself), aggregated per 100 m cell as scarp fraction, SNR-weighted amplitude, structure-tensor orientation coherence and dominant strike. Signature: a bidirectional strike-line neighbour test on per-cell scarp evidence (misses become isolated blobs; real fault scarps stay line-like). Why it can catch catalogue-missing faults: the catalogue is a Quaternary-scope compilation; older bedrock faults have degraded or buried expressions that survive in lidar micro-topography but not in the 100 m stack. Novelty: the sibling repositories use per-cell max-aggregated lidar channels and ridge/valley masks; no indexed prior applies a strike-consistency veto with an explicit expected-credit emission rule. Cost: done in this run; the native 1 m path is blocked by egress (see irregularities).

H2 — Expected-DTI greedy selection with per-dot marginal credit implemented, arm refuted on the instrument

Layers: any probability surface plus a per-cell truth-probability field. Signature: remove every dot whose single removal raises the plug-in expected DTI, using the exact shared kernel; the plug-in DTI is T/(0.2(T+FP)+0.8K) with T=Σ π·credit. Why it should help: the measured loss mode is redundant flank-shadow mass; the exact test targets it case by case rather than by a global spacing constant. Result: on the cross-fitted instrument this arm removed almost all dots and scored 0 on the surrogate truth — because the calibrated π under a heavily imbalanced surrogate labels the scarps as low-probability. That is a negative result and it is kept.

H3 — Cross-sensor corroboration veto implemented & measured

Layers: lidar scarp channels + detrended elevation curvature + radiometric texture. Signature: a cell must be supported by at least two independent evidence families (streaming second-best of ten normalised channels). Why: cultural and erosional false positives rarely appear in several independent measurements at once. Novelty: the indexed priors combine channels additively; a veto-style fusion is not indexed. Cost: low, folded into H1.

H4 — Curvature-polarity asymmetry (footwall/hanging-wall) mapping not runnable here

Layers: 1 m DEM (needed), or 10 m DEM as a degraded fallback. Signature: sign-consistent cross-fault curvature over hundreds of metres, with the fault dip direction inferred from the asymmetry. Why it could add faults: blind or buried faults express themselves as a systematic tilt of the hanging wall rather than a discrete scarp. Data needed: the competition's 1m_DEM_links.csv tile list and the USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar tiles — free and official, but the hosts are outside this sandbox's egress (checked 2026-10-11, see irregularities). Cost: high; requires an unrestricted runner.

H5 — Slip/length ratio screening against the Qfaults attribute table data available, not yet tested

Layers: Qfaults attribute table (5.8 MB, mirrored in the sibling repositories) plus a candidate surface. Signature: faults whose mapped length and slip rate imply continuations the compilation truncates. Why: compilation truncation and segmentation are documented mechanisms for “missing” faults. Novelty: the siblings evaluated gap-closure geometrically and refuted it on the live metric (T-v2: −0.0028); an attribute-conditioned version with the expected-DTI acceptance test has not been scored. Cost: medium.
