Candidate hypotheses before any submission decision

Expected improvement and cost are qualitative priors, not score projections. The checkout contains no feature data or spatial holdout. Only the top, lane-consistent hypothesis may be tested in this run; the others are parked research leads.

Scope and novelty caveat

This repository has no model or feature engineering, so all hypotheses below are new relative to this checkout. That does not prove novelty across the many sibling GEMSDOE sites. The user-supplied registry already names independent DTI-optimal emission, topography/geophysics, lidar-scarp, and dotted/spacing work. Before placement, the mandated registry correlation and within-3-pixel overlap checks must run on the actual surfaces/files. No claim of duplicate-free novelty is made here.

The session's fixed lane is the calibrated-surface, exact-kernel expected-DTI decision rule. Alternative feature hypotheses are documented for later prioritization, not implemented as a parallel model branch.

Priority ranking

The ranking is by qualitative plausibility of improving the competition's fault DTI, balanced against implementation cost and risk. It is not a numeric estimate of performance. The official feature list in the challenge describes detrended elevation/slope, strain-rate quantities, gravity, magnetic products and earthquake density; the actual local band order and cache are absent here.

First · active lane Calibrated structural-posterior ridges, emitted by exact-kernel marginal gain.
Layers: the shared cached feature stack only through spatially buffered out-of-fold ensemble/TTA/MC-dropout predictions; use isotonic-calibrated probabilities and ridge candidates. The physical signal is a coherent fault-likelihood ridge in the existing multimodal surface—not a newly asserted raw band.		 A linearly coherent ridge inferred from geophysical/terrain context may continue a structure beyond its mapped trace. Buffered hide-and-recover can test generalization to whole unseen catalogue segments. It remains only a proxy for genuinely new expert faults. Different decision rule from independent thresholding or heuristic spacing/MCLP: greedily maximize mean marginal gain under the exact 300 m metric across the uncertainty realizations, then prune. It is in the same broad family as earlier DTI-emission experiments, so uniqueness is unproved until the registry check. Blocked: no OOF surface, candidate list, evaluator, or registry is present. High qualitative potential because it optimizes the target metric directly; medium cost once shared tools exist. Unvalidated.
Second · parked Cross-modal magnetic/gravity edge coincidence.
Layers: reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly, total-field vertical/horizontal slopes, top-of-crustal magnetic source depth, isostatic gravity anomaly and its slope. Target a persistent, aligned ridge/edge intersection across independent potential-field contrasts rather than a thresholded raw anomaly.		 Fault juxtaposition or offset of magnetic/density contrasts can reveal a concealed structural boundary under basin cover where surface mapping is weak; independent modality agreement may prioritize a previously unmapped segment. Non-fault mimics include lithologic contacts, intrusive-body margins, basin-fill thickness changes and processing/flight-line artifacts. This checkout has no implementation; user-listed topo-geophysics and multiphysics runs may overlap, so global novelty is uncertain. Outside the active lane. Medium–high qualitative potential; medium cost. Not tested.
Third · parked Persistent lidar curvature/slope-break lineaments.
Layers: competition-linked 1 m USGS DEM tiles plus the provided detrended elevation and slope. Target multi-scale profile/plan curvature ridges and slope breaks that persist across scales; suppress drainage-aligned edges using local channel geometry.		 High-resolution topography can expose subtle scarps or linear breaks omitted from regional 100 m mapping, especially where a trace is not visible in lower-resolution feature layers. Non-fault mimics include stream incision, resistant lithologic boundaries, alluvial-fan margins, roads and grading. Prior site names explicitly include lidar-scarp/scarp methods, so this is not demonstrably new across sibling sites. DEM URLs and raster are absent from this checkout. Outside the active lane. Medium qualitative potential; high cost due to tile acquisition, mosaicking, multiscale transforms and edge artifacts. Not tested.
Fourth · parked Heat-flow residuals aligned with conductance boundaries.
Layers: USGS Great Basin heat-flow maps/well residuals, combined with challenge surface conductivity and depth-to-conductive-base products. Target a local thermal residual or conductance boundary that spatially coincides with an existing structural ridge—not simply high background heat flow.		 Hydrothermal circulation can perturb local heat-flow residuals and conductive structure around fluid pathways; these signals are independent of a surface fault catalogue and could flag blind structures relevant to geothermal systems. Non-fault mimics include groundwater advection, well-quality/coverage bias, lateral thermal-conductivity contrasts and non-fault alteration. A USGS ScienceBase item lists the heat-flow ZIP for download, so source availability was checked; it was not downloaded. Its license/reuse conditions and competition-rule permission to share with the sponsor still need confirmation before use. Target mismatch is material: heat-flow anomalies are not fault labels. Low–medium qualitative potential for fault-only DTI; high cost for provenance, licensing, alignment and validation. Not tested.
Fifth · parked Strain-tensor direction and earthquake-density coherence.
Layers: dilatation, shear strain rate, second invariant of strain rate, and earthquake density from the provided stack. Target a spatially coherent directional strain ridge with a local earthquake-density contrast, not broad high strain alone.		 Spatially organized deformation and seismicity may indicate an active structure whose surface trace is not in the public catalogue. Non-fault mimics include distributed regional deformation, swarm-like seismicity, catalog completeness changes and earthquake clusters unrelated to a surface fault trace. Inputs are already among the official feature families and may be present in existing baselines; per-feature AUC and provenance checks are mandatory. Outside the active lane. Low–medium qualitative potential; low–medium cost, but high leakage/confounding risk. Not tested.
