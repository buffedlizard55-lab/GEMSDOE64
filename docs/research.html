2.2 Why H33-2-B2 Achieved 0.2778 (ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED)

  1. Resolution of the Portal Range Validator Gate: The competition portal's array validator runs np.all((arr >= 0.0) & (arr <= 1.0)) on the decoded raster band before applying the footprint mask. Because NaN >= 0.0 evaluates to False in IEEE-754 floating-point arithmetic, rasters containing NaN outside the study footprint trigger "Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]" unless the portal masks by sample_submission.tif first. Writing 0.0 outside the footprint with nodata=None guaranteed 12,279,160 finite pixels in [0.0, 1.0].
  2. Exploiting the Asymmetric Tversky Index ($\alpha = 0.2, \beta = 0.8$) and 300 m Triangular Kernel: The organizer evaluation metric is the Distance-Weighted Tversky Index: $$\text{DTI}(P, G) = \frac{\text{TP}w}{\text{TP}_w + \alpha\,\text{FP}_w + \beta\,\text{FN}_w}, \quad \alpha = 0.2,\; \beta = 0.8,\; K(d) = \max!\left(0, 1 - \frac{d}{300\text{ m}}\right)$$ where $\text{TP}_w = \sum{x \in \Omega_{\text{eval}}} G_{\text{hid}}(x)\,\tilde{P}(x)$, $\text{FP}w = \sum{x \in \Omega_{\text{eval}}} (1 - G_{\text{hid}}(x))\,P(x)$, and $\text{FN}w = \sum{x \in \Omega_{\text{eval}}} G_{\text{hid}}(x)\,(1 - \tilde{P}(x))$. Because $\beta/\alpha = 4.0$, missing a hidden fault pixel costs $4\times$ more than a false positive pixel. Moreover, a single predicted dot $P(x_0)=1$ supplies $\tilde{P}(x) = K(|x - x_0|)$ across a $300\text{ m}$ disk ($\sim 13$ grid cells of $K > 0.33$), meaning a sparse lattice of well-spaced peak dots achieves nearly the same $\text{TP}_w$ as a dense ribbon while incurring a fraction of the $\text{FP}_w$ penalty.
  3. Why the B2 ($200\text{ m}$) Collar Pruning Improved DTI: Known mapped faults $\mathcal{C}{\text{known}}$ are excluded from the hidden test target $G{\text{hid}}$. Any dot placed at $d_{\text{Euc}}(x, \mathcal{C}{\text{known}}) \le 200\text{ m}$ sits inside the mapped fault zone where $G{\text{hid}} = 0$, so its central mass contributes $\text{FP}_w$ without discovering new unmapped fault segments. Pruning the 2,545 collar dots reduced $\alpha\,\text{FP}_w$ by $\sim 509$ weighted units while preserving 100% of the off-catalogue $\text{TP}_w$.

2.3 Why H33-2-B2 Was Capped at 0.2778 and How to Score Higher (Toward 0.3195 and 0.3774)

Despite its clean post-processing, H33-2-B2 has three fundamental physical and statistical limitations: 1. Zero Sub-100 m Geomorphic Resolution: H33-2-B2 relies on 100 m resampled predictors (training_features.tif). In the Basin and Range province (Great Basin / Surprise Valley / Dixie Valley / Carson Sink), Holocene and late Pleistocene piedmont and intrabasin fault scarps have widths $W_s \in [2, 20]\text{ m}$ and heights $H_s \in [0.3, 5.0]\text{ m}$. Box-averaging a $2\text{ m}$ scarp over a $100\text{ m} \times 100\text{ m}$ cell dilutes its slope break by a factor of $20\times\text{–}50\times$, rendering intrabasin and alluvial-fan scarps indistinguishable from background fan slope. 2. Cultural Lineament Contamination: In H33-2-B2, highway embankments (US-50, US-95, I-80), railroad grades, BLM mining claim trenches, and cardinal agricultural irrigation canals in flat playas generate linear topographic anomalies that mimic fault scarps. Our audit shows 5,021 of H33-2-B2's dots (13.33%) fall inside cultural road/claim/canal corridors where true off-catalogue USGS SGMC faults are depleted by $4.66\times$. 3. Isotropic / Uncalibrated Continuation: H33-2-B2 did not align local scarp strike with the NNE-striking ($\sim\text{N}12^\circ\text{E}$) extensional stress regime of the Great Basin or distinguish synthetic (basin-facing) and antithetic (mountain-facing) graben scarps from cross-slope fluvial terrace risers.

By introducing native 1–3 m lidar scarp channels (Scarplet + slope-aware detrending + Steger + Frangi), correcting the $90^\circ$ image-vs-geological strike frame bug, applying the physical + vector cultural-feature mask, and retraining under 4-fold spatial cross-validation, our lane achieves a +25.48% (+0.027821, $7.95\text{ SE}$) HOLDOUT-DTI gain in pure label-blind spatial CV (E1) and a +14.98% (+0.017000, $4.13\text{ SE}$) HOLDOUT-DTI gain in buffered whole-component spatial CV (E2), with statistically significant (> $2\text{ SE}$) improvements on both range-front and off-range-front blocks.

4. Mathematical & Physical Formulation of the Assigned Lane (H64-L1)

4.1 Scarplet Template Matching (Sare & Hilley, JOSS 2018) with Slope-Aware Templates (AGU 2015 T31A-2838)

Following Hanks (2000) and Sare, Hilley, & DeLong (JOSS 2018, doi:10.21105/joss.01066; JGR Solid Earth 2019), a fault scarp of vertical offset $H$ and morphologic age $\kappa t = \sigma_s^2 / 4$ evolving under linear hillslope diffusion on an inclined alluvial fan of regional slope $m_x, m_y$ has cross-strike elevation profile: $$z(u, v) = \frac{H}{2}\,\text{erf}!\left(\frac{u}{\sigma_s}\right) + m_u u + m_v v + z_0$$ where $u = (x - x_0)\cos\theta + (y - y_0)\sin\theta$ is the cross-strike coordinate perpendicular to geological strike $\theta$, and $\sigma_s = 2\sqrt{\kappa t} \in [2, 20]\text{ m}$ is the characteristic scarp half-width.

  1. Slope-Aware Background Fan Detrending (AGU 2015 T31A-2838): On coalescing alluvial fans (bajadas) in Surprise Valley and the Great Basin, the regional fan surface $z_{\text{bg}} = G_{\sigma_{\text{bg}}} * z$ ($\sigma_{\text{bg}} = 50\text{ m}$) dips toward the basin floor with unit down-slope vector $\hat{\mathbf{u}}{\text{bg}} = \nabla z{\text{bg}} / |\nabla z_{\text{bg}}|$. We subtract $z_{\text{bg}}$ to form the slope-detrended residual $z_{\text{det}} = z - z_{\text{bg}}$ and project the local scale-space gradient $\nabla_\sigma z_{\text{det}}$ onto:
  2. Synthetic (basin-facing) scarp step (downface_max): $\max(0, \nabla_\sigma z_{\text{det}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{bg}})$,
  3. Antithetic (mountain-facing graben) scarp step (upface_max): $\max(0, -\nabla_\sigma z_{\text{det}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{bg}})$,
  4. Cross-slope fluvial terrace/gully bank (crossface_max): $|\nabla_\sigma z_{\text{det}} \times \hat{\mathbf{u}}{\text{bg}}|$. Why this matters: Fluvial channels incising down the alluvial fan have gradients perpendicular to $\hat{\mathbf{u}}{\text{bg}}$ (crossface), whereas both synthetic and antithetic normal fault scarps strike sub-parallel to topographic contours and therefore maximize downface and upface!
  5. Scarplet Amplitude, Relative Age ($\kappa t$), and SNR: The matched-filter step derivative $\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\text{erf}(u/\sigma_s) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_s} e^{-u^2/\sigma_s^2}$ peaks at the scarp midpoint $u=0$, yielding the scale-normalized vertical offset amplitude $A(\sigma_s) = \sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma_s |\nabla_{\sigma_s} z_{\text{det}}|\,w_{\text{slope}}\,w_{\text{piedmont}}$, signal-to-noise ratio $\text{SNR}(\sigma_s) = A(\sigma_s) / (\sigma_{\text{noise}} + \epsilon)$, and morphologic youthfulness (inverse relative age $(\kappa t)^{-1/2}$) measured by the crest-to-toe Laplacian-of-Gaussian curvature dipole normalized by step height (scarplet_youthfulness).

4.2 Steger (1998) Sub-Pixel Ridge Detection and Frangi (1998) Multiscale Hessian Vesselness on the Slope-Detrended Step-Gradient Surface

A crucial mathematical insight verified in src/gems64/lidar_scarp.py: - For an error-function step $z_{\text{det}}(u) = \frac{H}{2}\text{erf}(u/\sigma_s)$, the second derivative of elevation $\frac{\partial^2 z_{\text{det}}}{\partial u^2}$ vanishes at the scarp center $u=0$ (its extrema lie at the crest and toe $u = \pm \sigma_s/\sqrt{2}$). - Conversely, the scale-space step-gradient surface $S_\sigma(x,y) = |\nabla_\sigma z_{\text{det}}(x,y)|\,w_{\text{slope}}\,w_{\text{piedmont}}$ is a 1D Gaussian ridge $S_\sigma(u) = \frac{H}{\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_s} e^{-u^2/\sigma_s^2}$ centered directly on the fault trace $u=0$!

Applying Steger (IEEE TPAMI 20(2):113–125, 1998) and Frangi et al. (MICCAI 1998, LNCS 1496:130–137) to $S_\sigma$ across scales $\sigma \in [2, 20]\text{ m}$: 1. Closed-Form 2D Hessian Eigensystem: From $H_{xx} = \partial_{xx} S_\sigma$, $H_{yy} = \partial_{yy} S_\sigma$, $H_{xy} = \partial_{xy} S_\sigma$, the eigenvalues ordered by $|\lambda_2| \ge |\lambda_1|$ are computed analytically in float32. Along a fault scarp ridge, $\lambda_2 \ll 0$ (strong negative curvature across strike) and $|\lambda_1| \ll |\lambda_2|$ (near-zero curvature along strike). 2. Frangi Multiscale Vesselness: $$\mathcal{V}(\sigma) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } \lambda_2 \ge 0, \ \exp!\left(-\frac{R_B^2}{2\beta_F^2}\right)\left(1 - \exp!\left(-\frac{\mathcal{S}^2}{2 c_F^2}\right)\right) & \text{if } \lambda_2 < 0, \end{cases} \quad R_B = \frac{|\lambda_1|}{|\lambda_2|},\; \mathcal{S} = \sqrt{\lambda_1^2 + \lambda_2^2}.$$ 3. Steger Sub-Pixel Curvilinear Centerline: Along the unit eigenvector $\mathbf{n} = (n_x, n_y)$ of $\lambda_2$, the directional first derivative is $r_1 = g_x n_x + g_y n_y$ and second derivative is $r_2 = \lambda_2 < 0$. The sub-pixel offset to the zero-crossing of the directional derivative is: $$(p_x, p_y) = t\,(n_x, n_y), \quad t = -\frac{r_1}{r_2}.$$ A pixel contains a verified sub-pixel scarp centerline when $\lambda_2 < 0$ and $(p_x, p_y) \in \left[-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}\right]^2$.

4.3 Aggregation to 100 m Cells & the $90^\circ$ Strike Reference-Frame Bug Fix

Every 100 m cell aggregates the native lidar channels into four primary features plus slope-aware/multiscale auxiliaries: 1. scarp_pixel_fraction: Weighted fraction of sub-pixels carrying active scarp lineaments (0.40 * frac_2m + 0.30 * frac_3m_g07 + 0.30 * frac_3m_g12). 2. snr_weighted_amplitude: SNR-weighted mean step height combining 2 m Scarplet step amplitude with 3 m multi-gate step height (h_med and h_gate07). 3. structure_tensor_coherence: Energy-gated orientation coherence $C_{\text{ST}} \cdot \frac{\text{step}}{\text{step} + 0.025}$ (suppressing noisy zero-gradient play-floor cells where raw coherence is random). 4. dominant_strike: Circular-variance-weighted geological strike in $[0^\circ, 180^\circ)$ clockwise from North. - Critical Bug Discovered & Fixed: In lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif, band 7 (strike) was stored as the angle from the image row/column axis (circular mean = 96.17°), orthogonal to the true geological strike of the mapped Great Basin faults (12.01° NNE). Applying the exact frame rotation $\theta_{\text{geol}} = (\theta_{\text{raw}} + 90^\circ) \bmod 180^\circ$ maps the circular mean to 6.17° (N6.2°E), turning negative strike alignment into a positive predictor (AUC = 0.5190).

4.4 Cultural-Feature Mask (Roads, Mining Claims, and Flat-Basin Agricultural Canals/Field Edges)

To prevent non-tectonic anthropogenic earthworks from consuming the dot budget, we construct a three-part cultural mask cmask (1,105,650 lidar cells = 28.40% of lidar domain): 1. Road Cuts & Highway Berms: tiger_road_distance_m < 75.0 m (TIGER/Line roads). 2. Mining Pits, Tailings & Exploration Trenches: blm_closed_claim_distance_m < 60.0 m (BLM mining claims). 3. Agricultural Canals, Levees & Field Edges: Flat basin floor (relief < 4.5 m), cardinal N-S or E-W alignment ($\min(|\theta - 0^\circ|, |\theta - 90^\circ|, |\theta - 180^\circ|) < 12^\circ$), double-edged embankment prism (ex_max > 2.0 * step_max), and zero 3 m far-field tectonic offset (h_gate12 == 0).

Crucial Modeling Finding: Including tiger_road_distance_m or blm_closed_claim_distance_m as positive linear features inside the classifier caused severe mapping-access bias (historic faults were mapped more densely near roads, so the model placed false positives along highways, dropping holdout $\text{TP}_w$ from 385.95 to 364.30). Using the cultural mask strictly as a feature & placement veto (cmask) eliminated road-access bias and improved both E1 and E2 holdout DTI as well as off-catalogue USGS SGMC fault recovery (0.115824 -> 0.118796, where cultural mask pixels are $4.66\times$ depleted in true SGMC faults: 0.241% vs 1.124%).
